The context

The research examines both model performance and interpretability: a fraud prediction needs to be evaluated alongside an explanation of the features contributing to it.

My contribution

Evaluated machine-learning models for credit card fraud detection and applied SHAP to explain model predictions. Served as first author and presenter of the resulting InCIT 2023 paper.

From problem to implementation

  1. Compare models

    Evaluated K-Nearest Neighbors, Random Forest, XGBoost, and Logistic Regression. The internship poster describes hyperparameter tuning and comparison before and after feature selection.

  2. Interpret predictions

    Applied SHAP to analyze feature importance and explain model predictions. The paper and poster provide the research context for this comparison.

Scope & credits

Research work, rather than a deployed fraud-prevention product. No production adoption, financial savings, or unpublished model scores are claimed.

Outcome & validation

Published in the proceedings of the 7th International Conference on Information Technology (InCIT 2023), with IEEE publication dated 30 January 2024. Presented at Mae Fah Luang University in Chiang Rai in November 2023.

Reading the evidence

Performance and explainability answer different questions. The study pairs model comparison with SHAP analysis so the evidence includes both predictions and their contributing features.
